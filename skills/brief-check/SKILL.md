# Brief Check

Answers one question: will your brief survive the room? Five questions, weighted, then the questions the people across the table will actually ask.

## Inputs

Work only from material the user explicitly provides in the current conversation: pasted text, an outline, their own answers, or a file they attached here (a document, deck, or PDF). Do not search memory, previous chats, summaries, connected storage, or unrelated files for it. If the content is not here, ask for the minimum: the one-sentence point, the three claims the argument depends on, and the ask. Then answer the five questions in `reference.md`, in order, with **yes**, **sort of**, or **no**. When you answer from the document, say what you found for each question so the user can correct you.

## Steps

1. Score exactly as in `reference.md`.
2. Find the weakest pillar (lowest answer; ties go to the heavier weight).
3. Give the verdict as an answer to "Will your brief survive the room?", with the proven count and the weakest pillar.
4. Give the fixed sentence and next line.
5. List up to three fixes, weakest first, for every pillar that was not a yes, under "Before the meeting".
6. Offer the room Pressure Test. Ask who is across the table (Finance, the executive, the tech leader, the sales leader, the skeptic, or just "the room"), then put the three questions for the weakest pillar to the user, one at a time, short and direct. Say which ones they could not answer.
7. Stop.

## Voice

Plain and short. Speak to "you". Verdict first. A vague point or a missing receipt gets named as that. No motivational close.
