# Rep Check: reference

Ported from quotabird.com/rep. Do not re-tune. A diagnosis in a fixed order, not a weighted score.

## The five questions, in order

| # | Pillar | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | TERRITORY | Could a good rep make this number in this territory, on this plan? |
| 2 | CUSTOMERS | Do customers choose to spend time with them? Do they get called back? |
| 3 | PIPELINE | Is there pipeline that exists only because they're here? |
| 4 | CRAFT | When they're in front of a customer, can they actually sell? |
| 5 | WILL | Are they still trying to win? |

## Diagnosis (first match wins)

| # | Rule | Say first | Sentence | Next line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | all five are yes | Neither. They're fine. | Leave them alone. | Don't invent a management problem because they don't love one-on-ones. Decide what visibility you need and let them sell. |
| 2 | TERRITORY is no | The territory. | Good rep, bad situation. | Nobody makes a number in a territory that can't produce one. Fix the territory, the number or the plan before you write anybody up. |
| 3 | CRAFT is no and WILL is no | The rep. Wrong fit. | Reasonable situation, wrong person. | They can't do it and they've stopped trying. Start the process. Six more months won't help them or the team. |
| 4 | CRAFT is no | The rep: a skill gap. | Good rep, skill gap. | They're trying and it isn't working. Name the skill, sit in their deals, work it one opportunity at a time. |
| 5 | WILL, CUSTOMERS or PIPELINE is no | The rep: an effort gap. | Capable rep, effort gap. | They can sell. They aren't. Expectations in writing, with dates, and a conversation about whether they still want this. |
| 6 | otherwise | Not clear yet. | You don't know yet. | Too many sort-ofs. Sit with them and go through five real opportunities, then run this again. |

## Moves (for questions that were not a yes, in order, up to three)

- TERRITORY: Size the territory yourself before the next one-on-one. If it can't produce the number, say so up the chain.
- CUSTOMERS: Ask which three customers would take their call tomorrow, then call one.
- PIPELINE: Split their pipeline into inherited and created. Put the created number on paper.
- CRAFT: Sit in their next two customer meetings. Say nothing. Watch.
- WILL: Ask them directly, this week, whether they still want to do this. Listen to the answer.

If everything was a yes: "Nothing. Tell them the forecast looks good and ask what they need from you."

## What your boss will ask

- TERRITORY: Has anyone ever made this number in that territory?
- CUSTOMERS: Which customers would take their call tomorrow?
- PIPELINE: What have they created this year that they didn't inherit?
- CRAFT: Have you watched them run a customer meeting?
- WILL: Have you asked them whether they still want to do this?

## Worked examples

Territory no, everything else yes: rule 2, "The territory. Good rep, bad situation."
Territory yes, Craft no, Will no: rule 3, "The rep. Wrong fit."
Territory yes, Customers sort of, Pipeline sort of, Craft sort of, Will yes: no rule 1 to 5 matches, so rule 6, "Not clear yet."
